Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Washington

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Washington crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Kitchen Cabinets

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Whether the water was clean supply water or gray discharge that needs sanitizing.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Appliance Leak Water Damage

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

We disconnect and move the appliance, confirm the supply is off, and trace the water path under cabinets, beneath flooring and into nearby walls with moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Standing Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

3

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.
