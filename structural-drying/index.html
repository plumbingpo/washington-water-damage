No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Washington Restoration know it well. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks

When to Call for Structural Drying in Washington

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

What to Expect From Our Structural Drying

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.
