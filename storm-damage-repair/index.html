How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install impact-resistant windows or storm shutters if you live in a hurricane-prone area
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • Ice dams form when attics run warm, so check that yours has enough insulation and airflow
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • Branches break in high wind, so keep nearby trees pruned away from the roof and siding

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Flooding in lower levels because drainage systems were overwhelmed
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Wind-driven rain coming in through windows, doors or wall penetrations
  • Ceilings or walls that stain while it is storming or soon afterward
  • Damaged or displaced flashing around roof penetrations, valleys or edges
  • Swollen ceiling panels or blistered, peeling paint in the days after a storm

Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. Our technicians trace where the water traveled using thermal imaging plus moisture meters, so no affected area is overlooked.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.
