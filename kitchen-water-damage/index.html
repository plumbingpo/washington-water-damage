Kitchen Water Damage Help in Washington

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Washington homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets

What We Do

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
