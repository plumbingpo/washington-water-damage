Bathroom Water Damage: What Washington Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. We help Washington homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

The cause and the terms of your policy decide that. A sudden, accidental event like a burst supply line is often covered. Damage from a slow leak, worn caulk or deferred maintenance usually is not. Check your policy or ask your agent. We document the source, the moisture readings and the affected materials with photos, which is what an adjuster typically asks for.
It comes down to what was leaking. Whichever fixture caused it, shower, toilet or tub, needs to stay off limits until the repair is made and things are dry.
