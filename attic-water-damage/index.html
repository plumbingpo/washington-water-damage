Attic Water Damage Help in Washington

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. In Washington, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Lost Insulation Value

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.
