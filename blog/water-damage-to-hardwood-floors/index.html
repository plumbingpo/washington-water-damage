Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Washington, DC

Home / Blog / Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Washington, DC

Few parts of a house are worth as much as its hardwood flooring, and few are as easily harmed by water. Boards that get wet can stain, buckle, crown, or cup, and mold may grow in the subfloor underneath. For Washington, DC homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

Why Hardwood Reacts to Water

Wood is hygroscopic, meaning it absorbs and releases moisture based on surrounding conditions. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. If moisture exposure continues, boards may crown (center rising higher than edges as the top dries faster than the bottom) or buckle (lifting entirely off the subfloor). Mold is another risk of long exposure, since it grows unseen between the subfloor and the hardwood and may not be found until the floor comes up.

Will Your Wood Floors Survive Water Damage?

Whether water-damaged hardwood can be saved depends on several factors: how quickly professional drying begins, the volume of water involved, how long the wood was wet, the type and finish of the hardwood, and the condition of the subfloor. In many cases, hardwood floors that receive professional drying within 24 to 48 hours of clean water exposure can be saved. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

How Professionals Dry Hardwood Floors

Professional hardwood floor drying uses specialized techniques that are different from standard structural drying. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Another option is to run a floor drying system under the subfloor, reaching it through the crawl space. The key is gradual, even drying, because drying too rapidly makes the surface dry faster than the core and leads to permanent cupping, cracking, and splitting.

When Floors Have to Be Replaced

Hardwood floor replacement is typically necessary when floors have been wet for more than 48 to 72 hours, contaminated water (sewage, floodwater) contacted the floors, extensive buckling or delamination has occurred, mold growth is present on the subfloor, or the subfloor itself is compromised.
