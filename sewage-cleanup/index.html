Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Washington, Washington Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How Our Sewage Cleanup Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Hazard Check and Containment

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

We extract every bit of standing sewage. Porous materials that were contaminated, such as drywall, insulation, carpet and padding, come out and go to proper disposal as biohazard waste.

3

Decontamination & Sanitization

We thoroughly clean every surface that remains, then disinfect it with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution. HEPA air scrubbers remove airborne pathogens and contaminants.

4

Dry-Out and Deodorizing

Commercial drying equipment removes all residual moisture. Sewage odors are eliminated through industrial deodorizing. Finally, a clearance test verifies that people can safely use the area again.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. Airborne pathogens and the chemicals used for decontamination can both pose health risks. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. Switching off the heating and cooling and the affected circuits is wise, provided you stay clear of the contaminated water while doing it. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
Typical pricing for this work in Washington is $2,000 to $10,000 and up, with the size of the affected area and the degree of contamination setting the number. A small backup that stayed contained might be $1,500 to $3,000, but widespread sewage flooding can pass $15,000 once replacement materials and reconstruction are counted.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Anything porous the sewage touched must go, which covers mattresses, upholstered furniture, drywall, insulation, carpet and padding. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
