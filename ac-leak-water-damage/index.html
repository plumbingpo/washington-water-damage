AC Leak Water Damage in Washington: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
