Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Washington

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Popped or exposed roofing nails, each one a tiny opening for rain.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Insulation in the Attic

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Lights and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

Trace the Leak Path

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

An exposed roof gets covered with a temporary tarp, or we line things up with your roofing contractor. Floors and belongings below the wet ceiling are covered as well.

After removing the insulation and drywall that are past saving, our crew dries the attic and lower rooms with air movers and dehumidifiers and tracks moisture levels until the wood is dry.

Exposed wood gets an antimicrobial treatment where needed. With the roof fixed, our team installs insulation and drywall, finishes the joints, and primes and paints so the repair blends in.

Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Mold, if present, and the number of days or weeks water was getting in.
  • Damage beyond the ceiling, such as wet floors, wall cavities or furniture.
  • How many square feet of wall and ceiling drywall have to be removed.
  • The amount of soaked insulation in the attic, and if it must come out.
